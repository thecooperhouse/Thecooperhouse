The title panels: “Alive?” is pure arithmetic — you are alive while your current points plus everything still on the table reach the leader’s total. In F1 that line has moved a long way: fourteen drivers and seven constructors are now mathematically out, with Liam Lawson sitting on the line to the point. In MotoGP the line crossed zero in Austria and now sits at 47, which puts ten riders out. “Title chance” is a Monte Carlo estimate over 200,000 simulated remainders of the season, and it is a model, not a fact. Its strengths are fitted so each entrant’s expected share of the points on offer reproduces their actual 2026 average, and it adds a per-round form shock, a per-race retirement probability and a season-long ±25% pace-drift term. It is deliberately not recency-weighted and it treats retirements as independent, which are its two biggest known weaknesses. Everything under about 1% should be read as “barring something extraordinary, no”.

Nothing about the model changed this week, and only one series raced. F1 had a weekend off, so its panel is carried forward from v10 unchanged; as a check, the simulator was re-run on the unchanged F1 standings and returned Antonelli 99.99% and Russell 0.011% against the published 99.97% and 0.026% — the same answer inside simulation noise, with Antonelli still winning 46% of simulated Grands Prix against his actual 57%. MotoGP moved, honestly and a long way: the leader's chance goes 49% → 67%, and for the second week running it refers to a different rider. Martín's Sprint win and second place on Sunday, against Márquez's second and fifth, turn a countback tie into a 12-point lead — worth roughly 20 points of title probability to Martín and 18 off Márquez — and with a round gone there is less time to reverse it. Bezzecchi drops from 3.1% to 1.8%; Pedro Acosta's first win lifts him to fourth in the table but not into realistic contention (0.02%). The MotoGP fit reproduces every rider's season average to within 0.17 points per round.

How to read the timing data: the gold chip next to the winner is their total race time; the chips next to P2 and P3 are how far behind they finished (≈ marks a figure reported to the nearest tenth or as "around X seconds" rather than official timing). "FL" is the fastest lap of that race and who set it — the driver/rider who set it wasn't always on the podium.

Circuit records: for F1 the "circuit best" is the official race lap record — the fastest lap ever set during a race on the current layout (F1's 2026-spec cars are around 2s/lap slower, so most records date from the 2018–2025 era). The Madring card is the exception: it is a brand-new circuit, so Sunday’s quickest lap is the record, by default. For MotoGP it's the all-time circuit record, which in bike racing usually falls in qualifying; where the record fell during a 2026 weekend the card says so.
