The title panels: “Alive?” is pure arithmetic — you are alive while your current points plus everything still on the table reach the leader’s total. “Title chance” is a Monte Carlo estimate over 200,000 simulated remainders of the season, and it is a model, not a fact. Its strengths are fitted so each entrant’s expected points per round reproduce their actual 2026 average, and it adds a per-round form shock, a per-race retirement probability and a season-long ±25% pace-drift term. It is deliberately not recency-weighted, which is its biggest known weakness right now: McLaren have won the last two Grands Prix and Aprilia have won three of the last six MotoGP Grands Prix and swept the Silverstone podium, and a form-weighted model would be kinder to both. Everything under about 1% should be read as “barring something extraordinary, no”.

How to read the timing data: the gold chip next to the winner is their total race time; the chips next to P2 and P3 are how far behind they finished (≈ marks a figure reported to the nearest tenth or as "around X seconds" rather than official timing). "FL" is the fastest lap of that race and who set it — the driver/rider who set it wasn't always on the podium.

Circuit records: for F1 the "circuit best" is the official race lap record — the fastest lap ever set during a race on the current layout (F1's 2026-spec cars are around 2s/lap slower, so most records date from the 2018–2025 era). For MotoGP it's the all-time circuit record, which in bike racing usually falls in qualifying; where the record fell during a 2026 weekend the card says so.
