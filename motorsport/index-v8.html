Paddock news & rumours

Snapshot compiled 31 August 2026 from official announcements and paddock press (motogp.com, Crash, The Race, Speedweek, Motorsport Week). Items tagged Rumour or One to watch are unconfirmed reporting. One story is spoiler-shielded because it draws on the Aragon GP weekend.

The title panels: “Alive?” is pure arithmetic — you are alive while your current points plus everything still on the table reach the leader’s total. “Title chance” is a Monte Carlo estimate over 200,000 simulated remainders of the season, and it is a model, not a fact. Its strengths are fitted so each entrant’s expected share of the points on offer reproduces their actual 2026 average, and it adds a per-round form shock, a per-race retirement probability and a season-long ±25% pace-drift term. It is deliberately not recency-weighted, which is its biggest known weakness right now: McLaren have won the last two Grands Prix and Marc Márquez has won three of the last five MotoGP Grands Prix, and a form-weighted model would be kinder to both. Everything under about 1% should be read as “barring something extraordinary, no”.

A correction to v7’s F1 numbers. The F1 drivers’ and constructors’ percentages have moved (Antonelli 99.1% → 95.6%, Mercedes 99.8% → 98.7%) even though no F1 race has been run since. That is a fix, not a result: fitted against raw season averages, the F1 field’s targets summed to more than the points a sprint-less Grand Prix actually awards — those averages banked six sprints, and only Singapore remains — so the fit could not converge and the leader’s advantage came out overstated. The strengths are now fitted to each driver’s share of the points on offer, which reproduces every target to within 0.05 points per round. MotoGP was never affected: every remaining round there carries a sprint, so its averages were always reproducible, and its method is unchanged.

How to read the timing data: the gold chip next to the winner is their total race time; the chips next to P2 and P3 are how far behind they finished (≈ marks a figure reported to the nearest tenth or as "around X seconds" rather than official timing). "FL" is the fastest lap of that race and who set it — the driver/rider who set it wasn't always on the podium.

Circuit records: for F1 the "circuit best" is the official race lap record — the fastest lap ever set during a race on the current layout (F1's 2026-spec cars are around 2s/lap slower, so most records date from the 2018–2025 era). For MotoGP it's the all-time circuit record, which in bike racing usually falls in qualifying; where the record fell during a 2026 weekend the card says so.

Data snapshot compiled 31 August 2026 from official series results, timing reports and press coverage. No F1 round has been run since the last edition — Zandvoort remains the newest race and keeps the shield; Monza is next, on 4–6 September. The Aragon round is cross-checked across the Crash, The Race, MCNews and Roadracing World classifications, which agree on every podium gap (+1.754s, +3.629s) and on the Sprint (+1.140s, +2.089s). Two disagreements are worth naming. First, the fastest lap: Crash’s results table prints 1:46.705 against Márquez’s name, but that is his 2025 race lap record — MCNews gives his actual 2026 best as 1:46.736 on lap 14, “0.031s outside his own record”, and 1:46.705 + 0.031 = 1:46.736, so the card carries 1:46.736 and the 2025 mark is not treated as having fallen. Second, Crash’s race report describes Bezzecchi as finishing “+1.8s” behind, which is his gap to Acosta rather than to the winner; the official classification’s +3.629s is used. Reports also disagree on whether Bagnaia’s lap-6 crash involved contact — his own account and Crash’s describe a solo loss of rear grip, so the card says that and nothing more. Every MotoGP rider’s total was checked against the Aragon classification point by point and reconciles exactly. F1 sprint rounds in 2026: China, Miami, Canada, Great Britain, Netherlands, Singapore. The Bahrain & Saudi Arabian GPs were cancelled in March, cutting the calendar to 22 rounds; on 26 July F1 confirmed the Bahrain GP will be run at Sepang, Malaysia on 2–4 October under the Bahrain name, taking the season back to 23. The season-closing Qatar and Abu Dhabi rounds are unchanged for now but not guaranteed — the FIA moved two WEC races out of the region in August and says it is still monitoring. MotoGP's Qatar GP moved to 6–8 November, which pushed Portimão to 20–22 November and the Valencia finale to 27–29 November; The Race reported on 28 August that it is now about to be cancelled outright with no replacement, which would make it a 21-round season, but that is not official and the tables here still count it. Aragon was round 13 of 22, so nine rounds remain. Zandvoort was F1 round 12 of 23, leaving eleven — with Singapore the only sprint still to come.
