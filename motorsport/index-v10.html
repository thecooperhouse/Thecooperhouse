Paddock news & rumours

Snapshot compiled 14 September 2026 from official announcements and paddock press (motogp.com, Crash, The Race, Speedweek, Speedcafe, Motorsport.com). Items tagged Rumour or One to watch are unconfirmed reporting. One story is spoiler-shielded because it draws on the Misano GP weekend.

The title panels: “Alive?” is pure arithmetic — you are alive while your current points plus everything still on the table reach the leader’s total. In F1 that line has moved a long way: fourteen drivers and seven constructors are now mathematically out, with Liam Lawson sitting on the line to the point. In MotoGP the line is still below zero, so nobody is out yet — one more round changes that. “Title chance” is a Monte Carlo estimate over 200,000 simulated remainders of the season, and it is a model, not a fact. Its strengths are fitted so each entrant’s expected share of the points on offer reproduces their actual 2026 average, and it adds a per-round form shock, a per-race retirement probability and a season-long ±25% pace-drift term. It is deliberately not recency-weighted and it treats retirements as independent, which are its two biggest known weaknesses. Everything under about 1% should be read as “barring something extraordinary, no”.

Nothing about the model changed this week, so the movement is all results. v9 rebuilt the simulator from scratch and warned that most of that edition’s shift was the rebuild rather than the racing. This edition runs the same code on new standings, which makes the comparison meaningful for the first time. The MotoGP number has moved enormously and honestly: the leader’s chance goes 71% → 49%, because the rider it referred to is no longer the leader. Marc Márquez takes a perfect 37-point weekend and Jorge Martín’s forearm gives out, and a 19-point lead becomes a dead heat on 274 with Márquez ahead on countback — the model now splits them 48.9% to 47.6%, with Bezzecchi on 3.1% and everyone else inside a rounding error. In F1 the leader goes 99.8% → 99.97%, which is the sound of a championship closing. Two sanity checks were re-run: the fit reproduces every entrant’s season average to within 0.04 points per round in F1 and 0.17 in MotoGP, and the race-by-race picture stays sane — Antonelli wins 46% of simulated Grands Prix against an actual 57% strike rate, so the model is still conservative about him.

How to read the timing data: the gold chip next to the winner is their total race time; the chips next to P2 and P3 are how far behind they finished (≈ marks a figure reported to the nearest tenth or as "around X seconds" rather than official timing). "FL" is the fastest lap of that race and who set it — the driver/rider who set it wasn't always on the podium.

Circuit records: for F1 the "circuit best" is the official race lap record — the fastest lap ever set during a race on the current layout (F1's 2026-spec cars are around 2s/lap slower, so most records date from the 2018–2025 era). The Madring card is the exception: it is a brand-new circuit, so Sunday’s quickest lap is the record, by default. For MotoGP it's the all-time circuit record, which in bike racing usually falls in qualifying; where the record fell during a 2026 weekend the card says so.

Five disagreements are worth naming. First, Antonelli’s win count: Motorsport Week called Madrid his seventh win of 2026, while formula1.com, Sky, GPblog and Total Motorsport all say eighth — the race cards on this page count eight, so eight is used. Second, Hamilton’s retirement lap: PlanetF1’s classification says lap 6, Sky, GPblog and Crash say lap 7; lap 7 is used. Third, Lance Stroll’s retirement: PlanetF1 gives brake issues on lap 12, Sky has him stopping in a run-off on lap 14, Motorsport Week says he hit the barriers at Turn 20 — lap 14 is used because that is when the virtual safety car that decided the race came out, and the card doesn’t claim a cause. Fourth, Motorsport Week places Carlos Sainz at Ferrari; he drives for Williams, and that report is not relied on elsewhere. Fifth, Crash’s Misano results page lists “fastest lap: Francesco Bagnaia, 1:30.877”, which is the standing 2024 circuit race record rather than Sunday’s best — MCNews gives Fermín Aldeguer’s 1:30.936 on lap 22, and that is what the card shows. F1 sprint rounds in 2026: China, Miami, Canada, Great Britain, Netherlands, Singapore — five run, one to come. The Bahrain & Saudi Arabian GPs were cancelled in March, cutting the calendar to 22 rounds; on 26 July F1 confirmed the Bahrain GP will be run at Sepang, Malaysia on 2–4 October under the Bahrain name, taking the season back to 23. The season-closing Qatar and Abu Dhabi rounds are unchanged for now but not guaranteed: the mid-September deadline Stefano Domenicali set has passed without a decision and October is now the expectation, with Imola reported to be on standby for a 6 December finale and nothing agreed. MotoGP's Qatar GP moved to 6–8 November, which pushed Portimão to 20–22 November and the Valencia finale to 27–29 November; The Race reported on 28 August that it is about to be cancelled outright with no replacement, but more than a fortnight later nothing is official — Carlos Ezpeleta says MotoGP is "trying to be able to go to Qatar" and will decide before the Asian tour opens in October — so the tables here still count it. Misano was round 14 of 22, so eight MotoGP rounds remain. Madrid was F1 round 14 of 23, leaving nine — with Singapore the only sprint still to come.
