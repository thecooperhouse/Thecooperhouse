Can the Broncos still make the eight?

Still alive on paper · one loss from formal elimination

Step 1 — the ceiling. Brisbane has 18 points with 6 games left and no byes remaining. Two points per win:

18 + (6 × 2) = 30 points maximum ← requires winning all six

Step 2 — how many have to fall. Thirteen teams now sit above Brisbane. To finish 8th, only seven of them may still be there at the end — so six must drop below. And four of the thirteen are already beyond reach:

Panthers 34 > 30 → out of range outright Roosters 32 > 30 → out of range outright Warriors 30 = 30 → tie on 30, but +190 beats −132 Sharks 30 = 30 → tie on 30, but +161 beats −132

That leaves nine catchable teams, and Brisbane needs six of those nine to finish below 30 points.

Step 3 — what each rival is allowed to do. Byes are guaranteed points, so a team's "floor" is its current total plus 2 for each bye still to come. That floor sets how many of their remaining games they may win before they lock Brisbane out:

Step 4 — the verdict. Not formally eliminated, but the shape of the required scenario has hardened. Brisbane must win all six and get six of those nine collapses — which means the Dolphins, Knights and Cowboys must all finish the season without another win between them.

Brisbane wins 6/6 → 30 pts Dolphins win 0 of 6 → 28 pts Knights win 0 of 5 (+bye) → 28 pts Cowboys win 0 of 5 (+bye) → 28 pts … plus three more of Souths / Bulldogs / Manly / Raiders / Storm / Titans finishing 28 or below

Put plainly: the Round 21 loss in Townsville cost Brisbane two points and cost the Cowboys nothing, so the gap to 8th went from six points to eight while the games available fell from fourteen to twelve. Three separate finals contenders now have to go winless for a month and a half, and three more have to fall over as well. Each is unlikely on its own; together they compound to something close to zero. The elimination is not yet formal, but the first loss from here makes it formal immediately — and Brisbane play the Knights, Dolphins, Raiders, Storm and Bulldogs, so they will be sending the results either way.

The one caveat that still bites: Brisbane's differential (−132) is the fourth worst in the competition, so ties don't help them. Reaching 30 points and drawing level with a rival almost certainly still leaves them outside the eight. In practice those six teams need to finish clearly below 30, not merely level with it — the only realistic exceptions being the Cowboys (−28) and Titans (−86), where a run of heavy Brisbane wins could just conceivably swing the tiebreak.

Looking to 2026/27

  • The Championship expands its play-offs from four teams to six this season — a meaningfully wider door for a club that finished 17th.
  • A new club crest debuts, reviving the 1977–92 badge: bottle kiln, Staffordshire knot, red-and-white stripes.
  • Stuart Harvey appointed technical director in May, joining sporting director Jon Walters in a restructured recruitment setup.
  • Mark Robins stays on for his second full season, backed by owner John Coates with real money — Stoke have been among the Championship's bigger spenders this window.
  • For Brisbane viewing: a 3pm Saturday kick-off in Stoke lands around midnight AEST.
