Latest result with Broncos in it — Australia 66, Cook Islands 6, in Townsville

World Cup round 3 · Sat 31 Oct · Queensland Country Bank Stadium, Townsville · 28–6 at half-time · Crowd 19,172

Australia66 – 6Cook Islands

Twelve tries to one, and the Broncos were in the middle of it. Reece Walsh, back at fullback, scored in the 31st minute, set up five tries and was named player of the match (ABC) — though he took a knock to the ankle he had surgery on late in the season, played through it, and handed the goal-kicking to Cameron Munster after three conversions (Yahoo). Kotoni Staggs scored in the 27th minute from Tom Dearden's pass and had another ruled out for a knock-on; Pat Carrigan started at prop. The Cook Islands led 6–4 early, before four Australian tries in 13 minutes made it 28–6 at the break; Jack Bostock and Tom Trbojevic both scored hat-tricks. Walsh: "I'm having a lot of fun at the moment." Walters said the knock does not put him in doubt for the semi-final.

  • The other Broncos men at the World Cup. Deine Mariner has not played since round 1. Tupou Francis made 29 tackles in Lebanon's 24–20 golden-point win over France, the last game of their tournament.   • The Broncos women at the World Cup. In her first game of the tournament, Jesse Southwell played five-eighth, scored in the 18th minute and kicked 20 conversions from 21 in the Jillaroos' 124–6 win over Wales — by NRL.com's account the biggest winning margin in Jillaroos history, and bigger than the Kangaroos' 110–4 against Russia in 2000. Her 44 points were two short of Ryan Girdler's Australian record of 46 from that Russia game, NRL.com noted. Tamika Upton scored twice. Julia Robinson is out of the tournament with a dislocated wrist from the England game (club, 27 October). Tavarna Papalii made 23 tackles in Samoa's 24–6 win over England, and Shannon Mato played in New Zealand's 40–12 win over PNG. The semi-finals are Australia v Fiji and New Zealand v Samoa — the second with Broncos on both sides.
  • Gosiewski may not be leaving after all. He is listed below as departing; Code Sports reports (via Zero Tackle, 27 October) that St Helens — coached from next year by Kevin Walters — are keen, and that the Broncos are open to keeping him for another year. Reported, not announced.
  • Sam Walker, the rumour. The Roosters withdrew a contract offer to their halfback on 26 October, and Trent Robinson says he will not play for them beyond 2027. Walker came through the Broncos' academy, and his father and two uncles played for Brisbane; pundits have tipped a homecoming, but News Corp reports the Broncos have ruled out a move (via Yahoo, 27 October). Treat it as a rumour.

A note on Riki's contract. The club's head of football, Troy Thomson, said in September: "Jordan is off contract at the end of 2027… He can't talk to anyone until November 1" (News Corp, via Zero Tackle). NRL.com's signings tracker (22 October) and Zero Tackle's 1 November contract guide both list him as signed for 2028. The club's own statement is the most direct source, so this page keeps 2027 — but if the lists are right, his window does not open until 1 November 2027.

Step 2 — what 2027 actually requires. An illustrative comparison using the 2026 structure, not a confirmed 2027 schedule. The NRL pays two competition points a win plus two for each of three byes, so a season converts cleanly:

comp points = (wins x 2) + 6 Brisbane 2026 8 wins -> 22 pts (14th) 2026 finals cut-off 13 wins -> 32 pts (Cowboys, 8th) Shortfall 5 wins Holding the 6–6 road record and fixing Suncorp: Suncorp 5–7 -> 11 wins = 28 pts Suncorp 6–6 -> 12 wins = 30 pts one win short of this year's eight Suncorp 7–5 -> 13 wins = 32 pts level with this year's eight Suncorp 8–4 -> 14 wins = 34 pts

The shape has not changed and it still cuts both ways. Going from the worst home record in the competition to a merely ordinary one only reaches the edge of the eight on this year's numbers — that is how far 2026 fell. But it is five wins, not fifteen, and the away half already happened: 6–6 on the road is a mid-table team, managed with an injured fullback, a retiring halfback and a defensive brief that changed hands in July. Keeping the two forwards on the November clock is the cheapest five-wins-worth of improvement available — and the club has now spent on a head of performance as well.

Confirmed versus rumoured player movement

Green Confirmed tags mean an announcement by a club, league or reputable outlet. Amber and red tags mark reported, speculative or collapsed moves. Where a label was inherited from an earlier edition without a source being re-checked this week, it has not been upgraded.

Not signings, however they are reported: Xavier Willison's extension is reported as verbally agreed (13 October) and, separately, as being tested against the market (18 October); Zero Tackle's 1 November guide says speculation points to him re-signing, but nothing has been announced.
