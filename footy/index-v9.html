Worth knowing

  • The review is in, and it says the problem was defence. Handed down Friday 11 September: Brisbane conceded 677 points, their worst since 2021, and spent most of the year without a permanent defensive coach after Ben Te'o resigned early following disagreements with Maguire. Matt Ballin now owns defence, Trent Barrett stays on attack, another development coach will be hired alongside Adam Brideson, and Troy Thomson becomes executive general manager of football. Pre-season is being rebuilt and travel restricted to Australia and New Zealand.
  • Maguire's future is settled, and it settled the ordinary way. He will see out the third year of his contract. CEO Dave Donaghy: "We will invest further in our coaching and development resources, supporting our head coach Michael Maguire, who last year became only the second coach in our club's history to win a premiership." Last week this page reported Brisbane were weighing an open-ended, clause-driven deal; that idea did not survive the review, and the club has simply backed the contract it already has.
  • Correction on Xavier Willison, and it matters. He is contracted through the end of 2027, not off contract now — so the "can talk to rivals from 1 November" line this page ran last week was a year early. That date is 1 November 2027. He declined Brisbane's July offer — reportedly $900k-plus a season to 2030 — rather than merely stalling on it, and James Hooper has since linked South Sydney with him for 2028, via the same manager who took Haas there. Souths have their own cap problems, so treat it as noise for now. The relevant fact is that Brisbane's best young forward has said no once already.
  • Reece Walsh is in Australia's extended World Cup squad, and that is not the same as being picked. He is one of 40 named in the preliminary pool, which will be cut after the Grand Final. He will arrive fit "but not by much", with no football since Round 24, and Dylan Edwards and James Tedesco are also in the squad after being left out of the Ashes side. Australia open against New Zealand at Allianz Stadium on Thursday 15 October, 8:05pm.
  • Payne Haas is having two fingers pinned. Surgery this week on the ring and little fingers of his left hand, which NRL Physio says he has been playing through since about Round 2. His World Cup — for Samoa, having switched allegiance last year — is in doubt. "I'll talk to the medical team this week and see what happens from there," he said after the Canterbury game. Missing the finals gives him a month.
  • A fifth 2027 signing: Wilson De Courcey. Twenty-year-old outside back from Newcastle on a two-year deal, 14 tries in 17 NSW Cup games this season at 126 metres a game, NRL debut in Round 5. He turned down a Knights extension after being dropped in Round 6. He replaces some of what Shibasaki and Arthars take with them, and follows Dane Gagai out of Newcastle.
  • Adam Reynolds has walked the rift back. Having told NRL 360 that his omission from the last two games was "news to him", he later accepted the call was his own and said the whole thing had been "blown out of proportion". Maguire, on 3 September: "We're working through what his role will be. He'll be around the club for sure… It would be crazy not to use Reyno." A halves-coaching brief mentoring Thomas Duffy and Jonah Pezet remains the expectation.
  • And the silliest item of the week. Melbourne lodged a formal complaint about Brisbane's ground announcer — former Bronco Alex Glenn — leading crowd noise while Storm players were under high balls at Suncorp. The NRL issued guidance to clubs. Glenn's defence: "It's harmless, it's harmless, I've been doing it all year… to every opposition." He is expected to keep the microphone in 2027. Given the home record, it plainly wasn't working.

Step 1 — the 677, checked three ways. The review's headline number is Brisbane's points against. It can be verified without trusting any single source:

Wikipedia Round 26 baseline : PA 657 over 23 games Round 27 (Bulldogs 20) : PA 657 + 20 = 677 Match-by-match list, 24 games, points against summed: 677 Review coverage (AAP), quoted figure: 677 Average conceded per game : 677 / 24 = 28.2 Points for 471 − points against 677 = −206 ✓ matches the published ladder

Three independent routes to the same figure. For completeness: Wikipedia's own match-by-match table sums to 460 points for, eleven short of the 471 its season summary and the ladder both require. One scoreline in that table is wrong somewhere in the middle of the year; every win-loss result in it is correct, so nothing on this page depends on it. The ladder figure is used.

Step 2 — the Suncorp paradox, resolved. Two figures were in circulation this week and both are true:

Home fixtures 12 games 2 W 10 L Away fixtures 12 games 6 W 6 L --------------------- Season 24 games 8 W 16 L ✓ Games played AT Suncorp Stadium: 12 home fixtures 2 W 10 L + Rd 11 Warriors (Magic Round) 0 W 1 L ← Warriors the home side + Rd 23 Dolphins (their home game) 0 W 1 L --------------------- 14 appearances 2 W 12 L ✓ = AAP's "lost 12 of 14" Closing run at Suncorp, any fixture : 9 straight defeats Closing run in home fixtures only : 7 straight defeats

So "2–10 at home" and "lost 12 of 14 at Suncorp" are the same season described with different denominators. This page keeps the 2–10 for the home record, because that is the number that goes on a ladder, and now carries the 2–12 alongside it, because that is the number that describes what it felt like to buy a ticket.

Step 3 — what 2027 actually requires. The NRL pays two competition points a win plus two for each of three byes, so a season converts cleanly:

comp points = (wins x 2) + 6 Brisbane 2026 8 wins -> 22 pts (14th) 2026 finals cut-off 13 wins -> 32 pts (Cowboys, 8th) Shortfall 5 wins Scenario A road form holds (6–6) and Suncorp merely breaks even (6–6) = 12 wins = 30 pts -> one win short of this year's eight Scenario B road form holds (6–6) and Suncorp goes 7–5 = 13 wins = 32 pts -> level with this year's eight

This is the honest shape of it, and it cuts both ways. Fixing Suncorp completely — going from the worst home record in the competition to an ordinary one — still only gets Brisbane to the edge of the eight, on this year's numbers. That is how far 2026 fell. But it is also five wins, not fifteen, and the away half of the season already happened: 6–6 on the road is a mid-table team, achieved with no defensive coach, an injured fullback and a retiring halfback. The gap is real and it is also closeable.

The honest summary. The review said the right thing about the right problem, named someone to own it, and did not pretend a premiership-winning coach two years ago is suddenly the whole explanation. Against that: the club conceded more points than in any year since 2021, won two home games, lost its best forward and its captain in the same off-season, and has already had its best young forward turn down a contract. Nothing gets tested until March, and Brisbane are not even in Las Vegas — the 2027 season opens there on 28 February AEST with Parramatta, Gold Coast, Melbourne and the Wests Tigers. Brisbane's own Round 1 fixture has not been published.

Seven games in — the sample is still small, but it is no longer meaningless

Seven of forty-six. Nothing here is a projection worth betting on, and this page will keep saying so. But three things can be stated precisely: the reconciliation, the Southampton deduction, and what Stoke's actual rate implies.

Stoke City: P7 W3 D1 L3 GF 11 GA 11 GD 0 Pts 10 -> 10th of 24 First 3 games : 0 pts, 3 scored, 9 conceded Last 4 games : 10 pts, 8 scored, 3 conceded Points off 6th (Charlton, 12) : 2 Points above 22nd (Derby, 4) : 6 Games remaining: 39 · maximum attainable: 127 pts

Two points off the play-off line and six clear of the drop, at the same time, after seven games. That is not a contradiction; it is what a Championship table looks like in the third week of September, and it is the reason nobody should read a promotion push or a relegation fight into it yet.

The rate, stated honestly. Extrapolation this early is close to worthless, but it does at least frame the two halves of the season so far:

Whole season so far : 10 pts / 7 games = 1.43 ppg -> 46-game pace 66 First three games : 0 pts / 3 games = 0.00 ppg -> pace 0 Last four games : 10 pts / 4 games = 2.50 ppg -> pace 115 Required from the remaining 39 games: 74 pts (a typical 6th-place cut) : 64 more = 1.64 ppg 50 pts (a typical survival line) : 40 more = 1.03 ppg

A 115-point pace is obviously nonsense over a season; so was the nought. The whole-season figure of 66 is the only one with any claim on reality, and 66 is a comfortable mid-table finish. The useful reading is not the projection but the requirement: to reach the play-offs from here Stoke need 1.64 points a game for the next thirty-nine, which is roughly a win every other match. They have just done better than that for four games. They had done considerably worse for three.

Southampton's deduction, recomputed — and it keeps shrinking.

Southampton on-field: W4 D2 L1 → 14 raw points, GD +10 Sanction: −4 points (spygate) Adjusted: 14 − 4 = 10 points Ranked on RAW points and GD : 2nd of 24 Ranked as published (−4) : 8th of 24 Net effect of the sanction : a 6-place drop Trend: v7 (31 Aug) 13 places · v8 (7 Sep) 11 places · now 6

Recomputed from the live table each week rather than carried over. The sanction is doing steadily less work, for the obvious reason: Southampton keep winning — 4–1 against Bristol City on Saturday — and a fixed four-point penalty compresses fewer places the further clear of the pack a club gets. On raw points they would be second. The v7 forecast that the swing would keep widening was wrong, was logged as wrong last week, and has now been wrong twice in the same direction.

The honest version. Four games unbeaten, three clean sheets in four, a first away win, goals from two of the three deadline-day signings, and 10th place with a game in hand on nobody. Against that: the four opponents were 12th, 6th, 21st and 17th, and Stoke have still lost to all three of the sides above them they have played. Saturday against Sheffield United, then three weeks to think about it, is a reasonable place to pause.
