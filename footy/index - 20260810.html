Worth knowing

  • Adam Reynolds, 36, announced in February that 2026 is his final NRL season — and he moves onto the Broncos' coaching staff from 2027. Concussion rules him out of the derby, so the farewell lap is on hold.
  • Payne Haas is confirmed for South Sydney from 2027 on a three-year deal, reuniting with Wayne Bennett. He has publicly denied any falling-out with Michael Maguire, saying the move is family-driven.

  • Michael Maguire is in his second year, chasing back-to-back titles from 16th on the ladder.
  • The underlying numbers explain the slide: Brisbane are averaging 3.8 line breaks a game in 2026 against the Cowboys' 5.6, and 1,545 run metres to their 1,759. The defence works; the attack has not.

  • Brisbane lead the Battle for Brisbane 6–1 and have won the last three, including 26–12 in Round 4. The Dolphins' one win was the 40–6 hiding in 2024.

Can the Broncos still make the eight?

Alive only on paper · Saturday settles it on points

Step 1 — the ceiling. Brisbane has 18 points with 5 games left and no byes remaining. Two points per win:

18 + (5 × 2) = 28 points maximum ← requires winning all five

Step 2 — the wall. Last week six teams were guaranteed to finish above 28. The Cowboys' win on Thursday, combined with their remaining bye, makes it seven:

Panthers 36 → beyond reach Roosters 34 → beyond reach Warriors 32 → beyond reach Sharks 32 → beyond reach Dolphins 30 → beyond reach Knights 28 + bye = 30 → floor locked at 30 Cowboys 28 + bye = 30 → floor locked at 30 ← NEW

Eighth place allows exactly seven teams above you. Those seven slots are now all spoken for. Not one of the remaining nine sides may finish ahead of Brisbane — and Brisbane must win every game left.

Step 3 — what the surviving scenario demands. Each rival's floor is its current points plus 2 per bye remaining. The last column is the most it can win and still finish at or below 28 with Brisbane ahead on differential — which, from −156, is a heavy qualifier:

Step 4 — Saturday is the guillotine. This is the cleanest number on the page. Lose to the Dolphins and Brisbane's ceiling falls to 26 — below the points both South Sydney and Canterbury have already banked, and they cannot go backwards:

Brisbane loses Saturday → 18 + (4 × 2) = 26 ceiling Rabbitohs floor → 26 (and +62 differential) Bulldogs floor → 26 (and −44 differential) ───────────────────────── 7 locked above + these 2 = 9 teams at or above Brisbane → eliminated on points

Win, and the thread holds for another week: 28 remains reachable, and the requirement becomes South Sydney and Canterbury both losing out — five from five, each — plus Manly winning no more than one, plus Canberra winning no more than one. Souths host the Eels on Saturday night and Canterbury play the Roosters tonight, so two of those conditions get tested inside 24 hours.

The honest summary: a side that has won six games all year needs five from five, and even that only reaches a number seven teams are already guaranteed to beat. It is not yet formally over — the differential tiebreak leaves a theoretical crack — but the practical version of "over" arrives at roughly 7:20pm on Saturday if the scoreboard is going the wrong way. The optimistic reading: the last five games are now purely about who Brisbane takes down with them, and three of the five opponents badly need the points.

Looking to 2026/27

  • The Championship expands its play-offs from four teams to six this season — a meaningfully wider door for a club that finished 17th.
  • A new club crest debuts, reviving the 1977–92 badge: bottle kiln, Staffordshire knot, red-and-white stripes.
  • Stuart Harvey appointed technical director in May, joining sporting director Jon Walters in a restructured recruitment setup.
  • Mark Robins stays on for his second full season, backed by owner John Coates with real money — Stoke have been among the Championship's bigger spenders this window. Robins has publicly set a top-eight target.
  • The window closes 1 September, a fortnight into the season, so the squad on Saturday is not necessarily the squad in September.
  • For Brisbane viewing: a 3pm Saturday kick-off in Stoke lands around midnight AEST.
