Worth knowing

  • Adam Reynolds' career is over, and he never got the send-off. He failed a fitness test before the Storm game and has been ruled out of both remaining fixtures — 15 seasons, 323 games, no final appearance. Dual lower-body problems (nerve issues in the left calf, then a right calf strain) and no meaningful training since a Round 22 concussion. His own summary: "I'm officially a has-been." He is expected to travel with the squad for the last fortnight and moves into the coaching staff in 2027.
  • The farewell itself was widely panned. Greg Alexander on SEN said the club "botched this completely"; Corey Parker said Reynolds "should have said he was playing for the last time". A pre-game tribute at Suncorp was clipped by an ad break — "you didn't even know there was a send-off, that's how bad it was." Last week's page noted Maguire and Reynolds telling different stories about the calf; this is how that argument ended.
  • Reece Walsh had surgery on a high-grade syndesmosis injury (Round 24, hip-drop tackle) in mid-August. Up to eight weeks puts him back around the second week of October — past Brisbane's season, and uncomfortably close to Australia's World Cup opener against New Zealand on 15 October, which he would enter with no match fitness.
  • The top eight is locked. Manly and Canterbury joined Melbourne and Canberra on the outside in Round 26. Nothing below eighth can now be changed, which is why Brisbane's last game is a dead rubber for both clubs.
  • Payne Haas played his last game at Suncorp on Thursday: "It's a very special ground. I'm gonna miss playing here." He joins South Sydney for 2027.
  • Xavier Willison's extension remains unsigned — no movement since last week's manager change stalled talks. Michael Maguire remains contracted through 2027 with the chairman's public backing.

One game left, and only two things it can change

Eliminated 8 AugWooden spoon impossibleCan finish 14th, 15th or 16th

Step 1 — the eight is shut. Every club has one fixture left except Newcastle, who have a bye worth a guaranteed two points. Eighth-placed North Queensland sit on 32 and cannot go backwards:

Cowboys (8th) floor = 32 pts Sea Eagles 28 + 2 = 30 < 32 → ELIMINATED (was alive last week) Bulldogs 28 + 2 = 30 < 32 → ELIMINATED (was alive last week) Storm 26 + 2 = 28 < 32 → eliminated Raiders 26 + 2 = 28 < 32 → eliminated Eels/Tigers/Broncos/Titans/Dragons → all well short

Last week two clubs below the cut were still breathing. Now none are, which retires the one genuinely interesting thing about Brisbane's final fixture: Canterbury have nothing to protect.

Step 2 — the spoon, revisited. Last week's verdict was that Brisbane were safe because the Dragons couldn't catch them. That still holds, and the Dragons' position has got worse rather than better:

Dragons 14 pts, 1 game → CEILING = 16 Titans 18 pts, 1 game → FLOOR = 18 Broncos 20 pts, 1 game → FLOOR = 20 Dragons ceiling (16) < Titans floor (18) → St George Illawarra are mathematically LAST, whatever happens → Brisbane safe by a margin of at least four points

So the 2026 wooden spoon is settled a round early, and it isn't Brisbane's. Cold comfort, but it is comfort, and it is arithmetic rather than hope.

Step 3 — 14th, 15th or 16th. This is the only live question left, and it is decided by two games: Brisbane at Canterbury, and what the Wests Tigers and Titans do. Both potential ties resolve on for-and-against, and both resolve the same way regardless of margin:

BRI win + Wests Tigers lose → both 22 pts gap = (−220 + m₁) − (−226 − m₂) = 6 + m₁ + m₂ > 0 always → BRISBANE FINISH 14th, guaranteed BRI lose + Titans win → both 20 pts gap = (−182 + m₂) − (−220 − m₁) = 38 + m₁ + m₂ > 0 always → BRISBANE FINISH 16th, guaranteed any other combination → BRISBANE FINISH 15th

Brisbane's six-point for-and-against advantage over the Tigers means a win-plus-Tigers-loss lifts them to 14th no matter how narrow either result is. The 38-point deficit to the Titans works exactly as ruthlessly in the other direction. A drawn game at Accor would leave Brisbane on 21 and clear of the Titans, but short of the Tigers — 15th.

The honest summary. Last week's page found a comeback win and a buried wooden spoon and called them small mercies. This week offers less. A 46–20 home hiding, a ninth straight loss at Suncorp, a club legend's career ending in a tracksuit after a send-off nobody could hear, and a final fixture stripped of the one bit of jeopardy it had. The only thing still worth watching is whether they can beat an eliminated Canterbury side and finish 14th instead of 16th. Take the win on Thursday and the season ends on a sentence rather than a shrug.
