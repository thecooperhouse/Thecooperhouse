Latest result with Broncos in it — Australia 36, Fiji 22, at Suncorp

World Cup round 2 · Sun 25 Oct · Suncorp Stadium · 12–10 at half-time · Crowd 41,108

Australia36 – 22Fiji

Closer than the score, and the first try belonged to a Bronco: Kotoni Staggs went through a hole from James Tedesco's short ball on 7', a try “at his home ground”, as NRL.com put it. Fiji had the better of the first hour by NRL.com's account, scored through Semi Valemei and Taane Milne, and led 16–12 three minutes into the second half. Tom Trbojevic's three tries in 15 minutes turned it, Valemei completed a hat-trick of his own, and Jacob Preston sealed it on 76'. Pat Carrigan came off the bench in the 28th minute and made 26 tackles in 45 minutes (NRL.com match statistics). Reece Walsh was a late withdrawal from the bench — “given the night off to rest ahead of the bigger games to come”, in NRL.com's team notes — and Tedesco, captaining Australia in his first Test since 2023, had three try assists. Fiji's Kurt Donoghoe was player of the match. Walters: "I think we need more from everyone. We know we can play better."

Worth knowing

  • 1 November now has three Broncos on it. On 21 October News Corp reported, via Zero Tackle, that hooker Blake Mozer — 22, contracted to the end of 2027, 16 NRL games — had been given permission to talk to other clubs. Two days later the club pushed back. Troy Thomson, to the Courier Mail: "There's a future for Blake at the Broncos, definitely… Blake is a free agent from November 1, but he is still contracted to us for next year and we are happy to honour that deal." Maguire: "I definitely do see a future for him here." That puts Mozer beside Xavier Willison, still unannounced after the conflicting reports of 13 and 18 October, and Jordan Riki, about whom this page found no new reporting this week. Six days to go, and no announcement for any of them.
  • Walsh: rested on the field, wanted off it. After an 80-metre try and more than 200 run metres in the opener, Walsh sat out the Fiji game. Off the field, The Daily Telegraph reports (via Zero Tackle, 24 October) that new recruitment boss Peter O'Sullivan — who helped bring Walsh to the Warriors in 2021 — has made a long-term deal for him his first priority. Walsh is contracted to the end of 2029, which NRL.com's signings tracker agrees with. Maguire: "We want 'Reecey' at the club for a long time." No offer has been reported; this is an intention, not a contract.
  • The other Broncos men at the World Cup. Xavier Willison started in the second row for New Zealand and played all 80 minutes of an 82–16 win over the Cook Islands in Christchurch: 157 run metres and 23 tackles without a miss (NRL.com). Deine Mariner is out with a hamstring injury — he scored on 2' against France, then lasted ten minutes. Tupou Francis came off Lebanon's bench and made 20 tackles in a 36–0 loss to Tonga. Ben Talty made England's final 23 but has not yet been picked for a game; Zero Tackle reports his maternal grandmother was raised in Staffordshire, which makes him the nearest thing this page has to a crossover. Two of next year's recruits are there too: Dane Gagai, rested by New Zealand on Sunday, and Shawn Blore, off Samoa's bench in both games.
  • The Broncos women at the World Cup. In the Jillaroos' 78–0 win over England in Perth, Tamika Upton had six try assists from fullback, and Julia Robinson, moved into the centres, scored her second try of the tournament (NRL.com). Jesse Southwell is one of three Broncos in the 23-player squad but has not been in the side for either game. Tavarna Papalii started at lock and scored for Samoa in a 76–0 win over Wales, and Shannon Mato and Brianna Clark both played in New Zealand's opening 18–12 win over Fiji. The Jillaroos' semi-final is at Allianz Stadium on 8 November, earlier the same evening as the Kangaroos'.
  • New faces for 2027. On 19 October the club confirmed Wilson De Courcey (21, two years, from Newcastle) and Richard Penisini (21, a development contract, from Parramatta). Four days earlier the NRLW side signed Tongan international Martha Mataele from Parramatta on a two-year deal. Details are in the ins and outs below.

A note on Riki's contract. NRL.com's signings tracker, updated 22 October, lists Jordan Riki as contracted through 2028. The same list does not include Preston Riki, a different Broncos forward whose re-signing in May runs to the end of 2028 (Zero Tackle). AAP reported in November 2023 that Jordan Riki had re-signed to the end of 2027, and the club's own 1 November statement only makes sense on that date, so this page keeps 2027 and flags the conflict rather than resolving it.

Step 2 — what 2027 actually requires. An illustrative comparison using the 2026 structure, not a confirmed 2027 schedule. The NRL pays two competition points a win plus two for each of three byes, so a season converts cleanly:

comp points = (wins x 2) + 6 Brisbane 2026 8 wins -> 22 pts (14th) 2026 finals cut-off 13 wins -> 32 pts (Cowboys, 8th) Shortfall 5 wins Holding the 6–6 road record and fixing Suncorp: Suncorp 5–7 -> 11 wins = 28 pts Suncorp 6–6 -> 12 wins = 30 pts one win short of this year's eight Suncorp 7–5 -> 13 wins = 32 pts level with this year's eight Suncorp 8–4 -> 14 wins = 34 pts

The shape has not changed and it still cuts both ways. Going from the worst home record in the competition to a merely ordinary one only reaches the edge of the eight on this year's numbers — that is how far 2026 fell. But it is five wins, not fifteen, and the away half already happened: 6–6 on the road is a mid-table team, managed with an injured fullback, a retiring halfback and a defensive brief that changed hands in July. Keeping the two forwards on the November clock is the cheapest five-wins-worth of improvement available — and the club has now spent on a head of performance as well.
