Worth knowing

  • Brisbane are set to become the first reigning premiers since the 2005 Wests Tigers to miss the following season's finals — a line that has been circling in the press for a fortnight and is now arithmetic rather than prediction.
  • Adam Reynolds, 36, announced in February that 2026 is his final NRL season, and joins the Broncos' coaching staff from 2027. He is listed to return in Round 24, so the farewell lap resumes — just with nothing on it.
  • Michael Maguire's post-match line was about building rather than the ladder: he admitted he did not think the side would be where it is, and pointed to consistency and culture over the closing rounds.
  • The Dolphins, meanwhile, are on the brink of their first-ever finals appearance — 5th on 32 points, and it was Brisbane who got them there.
  • The title defence started badly and never recovered: Brisbane lost the World Club Challenge 30–24 to Hull KR in February, then had Walsh, Haas, Hunt, Mozer and Jensen all miss extended stretches.
  • The underlying numbers explain it: Brisbane average 3.9 line breaks a game in 2026 and 1,531 run metres, against the Warriors' 6.2 and 1,745. Saturday was the season in one game — the attack can produce 24 points in 22 minutes and then nothing for 58.
  • Home has been the problem all year: 2–8 at Suncorp as the home side, 4–6 on the road. Two of the last four are at Suncorp.
  • Brisbane still lead the Battle for Brisbane 6–2 across eight meetings, but the Dolphins have now won two of the last five and both of their wins have come at Suncorp in the back half of a season.

It is over — here is the arithmetic

Mathematically eliminated · Confirmed Sat 8 Aug

Step 1 — the ceiling. Brisbane has 18 points with 4 games left and no byes remaining. Two points per win:

18 + (4 × 2) = 26 points maximum ← requires winning all four

Step 2 — the wall. Eight teams now have a floor above 26 — that is, they cannot finish on 26 or fewer no matter what happens:

Panthers 36 → floor 36 Roosters 36 → floor 36 Warriors 34 → floor 34 Sharks 32 → floor 32 Dolphins 32 → floor 32 Knights 30 + bye = 32 → floor 32 Rabbitohs 28 → floor 28 ← NEW Cowboys 28 + bye = 30 → floor 30 ───────── 8 teams above Brisbane's ceiling

Eighth place allows exactly seven teams above you. There are eight. Brisbane's best possible finish is now 9th, and that still assumes winning out. There is no differential clause left to argue about, no tiebreak, no scenario — this is the clean, unarguable version of elimination.

Step 3 — the exact moment. Two things had to happen on Saturday, and both did:

Time (AEST)EventEffect on Brisbane
~7:20pmDolphins 40 – 32 BroncosCeiling falls 28 → 26. Seven locked above; Souths and Canterbury sit exactly on 26, so a differential miracle survives on paper
~9:20pmRabbitohs 28 – 24 EelsSouths move to 28 — an eighth team clears the ceiling outright. Eliminated

Last week's page called Saturday the guillotine and put the moment at roughly 7:20pm. That was right about the mechanism and about two hours early on the timing: the loss did the damage, but it took South Sydney's win at Allianz to remove the last theoretical crack. Canterbury are still on 26 and could yet finish level with a Brisbane side that wins out — it simply no longer matters, because Souths made it eight.

The honest summary, and the optimistic one. A side that has won six games from twenty was never going to win four straight and leapfrog eight clubs; the maths merely caught up with the football. What is left is genuinely worth watching: four opponents who all need something, a fit-again Adam Reynolds getting a proper send-off rather than a concussion protocol, and Ezra Mam producing a hat-trick in a losing side at 23 years old. The 2027 roster — Barnett, Pezet, Hunt, Reynolds on the coaching staff — is being assembled in plain sight. This season's job now is to be the team nobody in the eight wants to draw in the last month.

Stoke City2 – 0Oldham

Workmanlike rather than convincing, and Mark Robins said as much. Goalless at the break, with Viktor Johansson — wearing the captain's armband — making a smart double save from Mike Fondop and denying debutant Keenan Appiah-Forson. Stoke have finished between 14th and 18th in eight straight seasons — the streak this squad is being paid to break, and the 50th second-tier season is a milestone the club would rather not be marking.

Looking to 2026/27

  • The Championship expands its play-offs from four teams to six this season — a meaningfully wider door for a club that finished 17th.
  • A new club crest debuts, reviving the 1977–92 badge: bottle kiln, Staffordshire knot, red-and-white stripes. It got its first competitive outing on Saturday.
  • Stuart Harvey appointed technical director in May, joining sporting director Jon Walters in a restructured recruitment setup.
  • Mark Robins stays on for his second full season, backed by owner John Coates with real money — Stoke have been among the Championship's bigger spenders this window. Robins has publicly set a top-eight target.
  • The window closes 31 August, a fortnight into the season, so the squad on Saturday is not necessarily the squad in September.
  • Stoke last won this trophy in 1972, beating Chelsea 2–1 at Wembley. They went out in the second round to Bradford City last season, so Saturday already improves on the 2025/26 cup run in terms of rounds survived at the first attempt.
  • For Brisbane viewing: a 3pm Saturday kick-off in Stoke lands around midnight AEST.

Squad shape

  • Sorba Thomas — last season's engine and top scorer with 10 goals. Assist totals genuinely disagree across databases: Soccerway logs 10 in 40 league appearances, while the Daily Mail and VAVEL both report 13 in 45. Contracted to June 2028, valued around £9–10m, still the name most linked with a move — and still starting, and still taking the corners.
  • Viktor Johansson — captained the side on Saturday, kept the clean sheet, and retains the number 1 shirt despite Josh Griffiths arriving. Returned from a World Cup summer as Sweden's understudy to Derby's Jacob Zetterström.
  • Róbert Boženík — switched to the number 9 shirt for 26/27 and scored the second off the bench.
  • Million Manhoef — 7 goals, 4 assists last season, another ever-present campaign; started wide right on Saturday and was withdrawn after an hour.
  • Lamine Cissé — worth remembering he is not a new signing. The Frenchman arrived from Bastia in August 2025 for around £2m and lost much of last season to a shoulder injury; Saturday was a start in the front three.
  • Ashley Phillips — two seasons on loan from Spurs and arguably one of the division's best defenders in that time, but Stoke lost the race. Middlesbrough had agreed a fee reported at £7m before talks stalled on 9 August. Luke Graham is the in-house answer, and he started at centre-half on Saturday.
