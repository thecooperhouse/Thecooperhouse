Worth knowing

  • Reece Walsh's season is over. High-grade syndesmosis, surgery on Monday, an eight-week window that points to early October. That timeline also puts his Rugby League World Cup campaign in real doubt.
  • Brisbane are set to become the first defending premiers since the 2006 Wests Tigers to miss the finals the year after winning the title. (The Tigers won the 2005 premiership and finished 10th in 2006; Canterbury did the same in 2005 after winning in 2004.)
  • Adam Reynolds, 36, retires at season's end and joins the Broncos' coaching staff in 2027. The soleus strain is graded one and he told reporters it will be “tough to get there, but I'll be doing everything possible” for Round 26 at Suncorp — his last realistic home farewell.
  • With Reynolds out, Pat Carrigan is captaining the side, and it was Carrigan being asked post-match whether motivation was a problem with the finals gone — a question Maguire stepped in to answer himself.
  • The Dolphins' rise continues: 4th on 34 points and heading for their first finals series. Brisbane's Round 23 collapse against them is a large part of the reason.
  • The title defence started badly and never recovered: a 30–24 World Club Challenge loss to Hull KR in February, then Walsh, Haas, Hunt, Mozer and Jensen all missing extended stretches. Walsh's ankle is the closing bookend.
  • Home has been the problem all year: 2–9 at Suncorp as the home side against 4–6 on the road. One home game remains.
  • Ezra Mam's Round 23 hat-trick against the Dolphins is still the season's best individual hour. He shifted to five-eighth alongside Thomas Duffy on Saturday and Brisbane managed one try.

The season is gone — here is what the last three games are actually worth

Eliminated 8 Aug · Wooden spoon still mathematically live

Step 1 — the ceiling. Brisbane have 18 points, three games left and no byes remaining:

18 + (3 × 2) = 24 points maximum ← requires winning out

Eighth place is currently 28 points and the Cowboys still have a bye banked, so their floor is 30. Brisbane cannot reach 30. That has been settled since 8 August and nothing on the weekend changed it.

Step 2 — the one that is still live: the spoon. The Dragons have won two in a row and are on 14. Two results now matter at the bottom:

Broncos 18, 3 games, 0 byes → ceiling 24, floor 18 Dragons 14, 3 games, 0 byes → ceiling 20, floor 14 Titans 18, 3 games, 0 byes → ceiling 24, floor 18 (+44 on differential)
ScenarioBrisbane finish onResult
Lose all three, Dragons win all three18 v Dragons' 2017th — wooden spoon
Win one20 — level with the Dragons' absolute ceiling, and 65 points clear on differentialSafe in all realistic cases
Win two22 — above the Dragons' ceilingArithmetically impossible to finish last

St George Illawarra have won four games all year, so this is not a likely outcome — but it is a real one, and it is the only ladder arithmetic Brisbane still control. One win removes it.

Step 3 — the kingmaker role, verified. Eight clubs now have a floor of 30 or better: Roosters 38, Panthers 36, Warriors 36, Dolphins 34, Knights 34 (32 + bye), Sharks 32, Rabbitohs 30, Cowboys 30 (28 + bye). Eighth place therefore requires a ceiling of at least 30. Only four clubs below the line clear it:

Bulldogs 26 + 6 = 32 ← alive outright · Brisbane away, Rd 27 Sea Eagles 24 + 6 = 30 ← alive on differential Storm 24 + 6 = 30 ← alive on differential · Brisbane home, Rd 26 Raiders 24 + 2 bye + 4 = 30 ← alive on diff · Brisbane away, Rd 25 Eels 22 + 6 = 28 ← eliminated

Three of those four play Brisbane. Canberra on Friday, Melbourne at Suncorp the following Thursday, Canterbury in the final round — and Canterbury's Round 27 game against Brisbane may well be the last hurdle between them and eighth. A side with nothing to play for gets to decide quite a lot about who else does.

The honest summary, and the optimistic one. This was the worst weekend of a bad season: 40–6 at home in front of 38,916, the fullback carried off, the captain in a moon boot's worth of calf strain, and a coach reduced to four words. There is no dressing that up. What is left is three games in which Brisbane can be extremely inconvenient to three clubs who need the points, plus one home night at Suncorp to send Adam Reynolds off properly. The 2027 roster — Barnett, Pezet, Hunt, Reynolds on the coaching staff, Walsh back from surgery for a full pre-season — is being assembled in plain sight. It is a small thing to play for, but it is not nothing.

Looking to 2026/27

  • The Championship expands its play-offs from four teams to six this season, with eliminators for the sides finishing 3rd–8th feeding into semi-finals — a meaningfully wider door for a club that finished 17th.
  • A new club crest debuts, reviving the 1977–92 badge: bottle kiln, Staffordshire knot, red-and-white stripes.
  • Stuart Harvey appointed technical director in May, joining sporting director Jon Walters in a restructured recruitment setup.
  • Mark Robins stays on for his second full season, backed by owner John Coates with real money — Stoke have been among the Championship's bigger spenders this window. Robins has publicly set a top-eight target, which the expanded play-offs now make worth something.
  • The window closes 31 August, a fortnight into the season, and Robins says recruitment staff have been working “round the clock”. The squad on Saturday is not necessarily the squad in September.
  • Stoke last won this trophy in 1972, beating Chelsea 2–1 at Wembley — still the club's only major honour.
  • For Brisbane viewing: a 3pm Saturday kick-off in Stoke lands around midnight AEST; the 12:30pm at Wolves is a civilised 9:30pm.
