Worth knowing

  • Willison won the Paul Morgan Medal, and said the thing Brisbane needed him to say. At the club awards on 14 September: "I definitely want to stay. They have given me everything. I love the culture… My daughter was born in Brisbane so I would love to stay here and bring my family up here." He played all 24 games at 124 metres a game with seven tries. Brisbane are reported to be in advanced talks over a seven-figure deal that could run to 2031, using the room Haas's exit creates. Also honoured: Pat Carrigan (players' player), Deine Mariner (best back), Va'a Semu (rookie of the year), Jordan Riki (most consistent).
  • This page got the Willison date wrong last week, and it is worth being precise about how. v9 "corrected" the free-agency date from 1 November 2026 to 1 November 2027, reasoning that a player contracted through 2027 could not be talking to rivals a year early. That reasoning was backwards. Under the RLPA/NRL contracting window, a player may negotiate with other clubs once he enters the final twelve months of his deal — which, for a contract expiring at the end of 2027, is 1 November 2026. The original date was right; the correction introduced the error. It is fixed here and the working is in the maths panel.
  • Jaiyden Hunt is going to Canberra, and Jack Gosiewski is going somewhere. Hunt has signed a two-year deal reported at $350,000 with the Raiders, announced 14 September — Canberra are rebuilding a pack that has lost Papali'i to retirement and Horsburgh and Smithies to Perth. Gosiewski's contract will not be renewed and no destination has been reported. Both were listed here last week as "off contract — undecided"; both have now resolved, one of them only halfway. The club also farewelled Hoeter, Rogers and Te Kura at its awards night; see the updated list below.
  • Reece Walsh has not joined the NFL. He has signed an ambassadorial deal with the Seattle Seahawks — one of the first signings to their "12s Everywhere" programme in this region, alongside Canberra's Hudson Young — as the NFL pushes into Australia. He remains contracted to Brisbane through 2029. Several headlines this week read "from the NRL to the NFL", which is doing a great deal of work for a marketing partnership.
  • The actual Walsh question is still his ankle. No football since Round 24, syndesmosis surgery in mid-August, named in Australia's extended 40-man World Cup pool which gets cut after the Grand Final. Nothing this week moved that timeline either way. Australia open against New Zealand at Allianz Stadium on Thursday 15 October, 8:05pm.
  • Payne Haas: no news, which is itself the news. The last substantive update remains 4 September — two fingers on his left hand to be pinned, carried since roughly Round 2, World Cup with Samoa in doubt. Samoa's squad has not been named. The tournament starts in 24 days.
  • Dally M night is Wednesday 30 September. Nathan Cleary leads the men's count and is a heavy favourite; no Brisbane man is in the published top ten. Tamika Upton is favoured to retain the women's medal, and on Saturday's evidence it would be hard to argue.
  • And the part of the club that is still playing is playing very well. The NRLW side, defending premiers, finished third, won their semi-final by 24 and now travel to the Gold Coast on Sunday for a preliminary final against the Titans, who finished second. Win that and they are back at Accor Stadium on 4 October, chasing back-to-back titles and a fifth overall.

Three clocks, one ladder that cannot move, and a correction to a correction

Men's season over · 14th · 8–16NRLW: one win from a grand final41 days to 1 November

Step 1 — the free-agency clock, done properly this time. This is the number this page got wrong last week, so here is the whole chain rather than the conclusion:

Rule (RLPA contracting window): a player may negotiate with rival clubs once he enters the FINAL TWELVE MONTHS of his contract. Willison's contract expires : end of 2027 Final twelve months begin : 1 November 2026 Rivals may talk to him from : 1 November 2026 Days from snapshot (21 Sep) : 41 v8 said 1 Nov 2026 -> correct v9 "corrected" to 2027 -> wrong; the reasoning inverted the rule v10 restores 1 Nov 2026 and shows the rule

The trap was an intuitive one: he is contracted for all of 2027, so it feels as though he cannot be courted until that year is nearly done. The rule is the opposite — the window opens before the final season, not after it. Which is why Brisbane are trying to get this signed in the next six weeks rather than next spring.

Step 2 — what 2027 actually requires. An illustrative comparison using the 2026 structure, not a confirmed 2027 schedule: the NRL pays two competition points a win plus two for each of three byes, so a season converts cleanly:

comp points = (wins x 2) + 6 Brisbane 2026 8 wins -> 22 pts (14th) 2026 finals cut-off 13 wins -> 32 pts (Cowboys, 8th) Shortfall 5 wins Holding the 6–6 road record and fixing Suncorp: Suncorp 5–7 -> 11 wins = 28 pts Suncorp 6–6 -> 12 wins = 30 pts one win short of this year's eight Suncorp 7–5 -> 13 wins = 32 pts level with this year's eight Suncorp 8–4 -> 14 wins = 34 pts

The shape of it has not changed and it still cuts both ways. Going from the worst home record in the competition to a merely ordinary one still only reaches the edge of the eight on this year's numbers — that is how far 2026 fell. But it is five wins, not fifteen, and the away half of the season already happened: 6–6 on the road is a mid-table team, managed with no defensive coach, an injured fullback and a retiring halfback. Keeping the player who just won the club's medal is the cheapest five-wins-worth of improvement available.

Step 3 — the Grand Final clock, because two different times are being published and both are right.

Published as 7:30pm AEDT (Sydney) and 6:30pm AEST (Brisbane) NSW switches to daylight saving : Sun 4 Oct 2026, 2:00am (first Sunday in October) Queensland switches : never 7:30pm AEDT = 6:30pm AEST = the same kick-off This page prints 6:30pm, because that is the clock in Brisbane.

The Grand Final is on the very morning the change takes effect, which is exactly the sort of date that produces two "correct" times in two publications and an argument in a group chat. Both preliminary finals are before the switch, so their AEST times are unambiguous.

The honest summary. The men's season ended three weeks ago and nothing since has changed the shape of it: 14th, 677 conceded, two home wins. What this week did add is genuinely positive and should be said plainly — the best young forward at the club won its highest individual honour and publicly said he wants to stay, and the talks are reportedly advanced rather than stalled. Set against that: the departure list is now updated below, the fullback's ankle is still an open question, and the preliminary final at Suncorp on Friday belongs to Redcliffe. The women's side, meanwhile, is 70 minutes from another grand final, which is the single most cheerful sentence on this page.
